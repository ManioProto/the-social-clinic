For people using GLP-1 medicines

When Wanting Changes

Public course · in development

The medication can change your appetite. It cannot tell you what to do with that change.

GLP-1 medicines can make food quieter, hunger different and weight loss possible in ways that once felt unreachable. But biological change can also unsettle habits, pleasure, identity, relationships and the stories we have been taught about willpower and body size.

When Wanting Changes is being designed for people who want to understand that experience — without diet culture, miracle claims or pharmaceutical marketing.

It will explore food noise, appetite and desire; habit without hunger; body change and identity; stigma and deservingness; relationships; maintenance; and what it can mean to live in a body that is changing faster than the story you have about it.

This is not medical advice and it will not tell you what to take or how to take it. It is education about the experience around treatment.

Beyond Weight

A Social Clinic on GLP-1 medicines, appetite, stigma and the clinical encounter

3 weeks3 × 60 minutesLive & onlineMaximum 18 participantsNZ$395 incl. GST

GLP-1 medicines have changed what medicine can do about weight. The clinical encounter has inherited everything else.

GLP-1 medicines have unsettled some remarkably persistent ideas about appetite, effort, willpower, responsibility and what successful weight loss is supposed to look like. Beyond Weight brings contemporary clinical evidence into conversation with the psychological, social and cultural dimensions of weight and weight-loss treatment.

This is not a prescribing course. It is a course about what prescribing encounters.

Why The Social Clinic?

Biology matters.

So do biography, culture, institutions, relationships, stigma, desire and the social world.

Healthcare increasingly has extraordinary capacity to intervene in biology. But biological change occurs in people whose lives cannot be reduced to biomarkers, diagnoses or prescriptions.

The Social Clinic creates professional education for the territory between them.

Evidence without reduction.
Complexity without obscurity.
Theory that earns its place in practice.

Course director

Dr Andrew Dickson

Social theory made clinically useful.

Andrew is an academic, writer and educator whose work crosses health, organisation studies, sociology and psychoanalytic theory.

His doctoral research examined the psychological and social dimensions of weight loss, appetite and weight-anxiety, including his own experience of pharmacologically mediated weight loss. His broader work explores how institutions, professional knowledge, culture and social structures shape people’s experiences of health and healthcare.

He founded The Social Clinic to create rigorous, independent professional education in the parts of clinical practice that biology alone cannot explain.

The aim is not to make clinicians into social scientists. It is to make social science clinically useful.

Evidence & independence

Independent by design.

Programmes draw on peer-reviewed research, contemporary clinical evidence and relevant scholarship from the social sciences and humanities. Evidence, uncertainty and competing interpretations are distinguished explicitly.

No pharmaceutical sponsorship. No medicine or manufacturer promoted. No commercial influence over programme content.

Because good clinical education should be able to ask questions about medicine without having to sell one.
