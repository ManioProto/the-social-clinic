Beyond Weight

For decades medicine told patients to eat less and try harder. Now it has drugs that work, and the old script is still sitting in the consulting room.

Beyond Weight is a three-week course about what happens once the prescription is written. Your patients arrive carrying years of advice about effort and willpower. So do you. Most of it no longer fits the pharmacology, and very little of it has gone away.

You won't learn to prescribe here. Your college already covers that. This course deals with what the prescription stirs up.

How it runs

Bring the clinic with you.

Each session opens a problem. You take a small observation back into your practice, then bring what you noticed to the next session. It works best if you're seeing patients while you do it.

Week one: How did we get here?

Weight-loss medicine has spent a century deciding where the problem lives. The body, the appetite, the environment, and more often than anyone admits, the patient's character. We look at how that history still shapes what gets said across the desk.

Between sessions: listen to how you and your patients talk about effort.

Week two: When wanting changes

Patients describe the food noise going quiet. Some are relieved. Some discover the noise was doing a job for them. This session covers stigma, the belief that weight loss has to be earned, and what people are really asking the drug to fix.

Between sessions: notice the gap between what a patient asks for and what they seem to want.

Week three: After the prescription

Here we put the biology back with the person: culture, expectations, goals, and the assumptions you bring into the room yourself. The last question is practical. What will you do differently on Monday?

Why this course exists

The lag matters.

Medicine can now change a body quickly. The person living in it catches up more slowly, and sometimes never does.

A lot of treatment quietly goes wrong in that lag, and most clinical training stops just short of it.

Every piece of theory in this course has to be useful in a fifteen-minute appointment. If it isn't, I cut it.

About me

Dr Andrew Dickson

I'm Dr Andrew Dickson, an academic and writer working across health, sociology, organisation studies and psychoanalysis. I teach at the University of Otago and write regularly for The Spinoff, Newsroom, RNZ and Stuff.

My doctorate looked at weight loss, appetite and weight anxiety from the inside, partly through my own experience of losing weight with the help of medication. I started The Social Clinic because the hardest questions about GLP-1 treatment turn out to be social and psychological, and clinicians are being left to answer them alone.
