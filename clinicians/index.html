A Social Clinic on GLP-1 medicines, appetite, stigma and the clinical encounter

GLP-1 medicines have unsettled persistent ideas about appetite, effort, willpower, responsibility and what successful weight loss is supposed to look like. Beyond Weight brings contemporary clinical evidence into conversation with the psychological, social and cultural dimensions of weight and weight-loss treatment.

Course in development. RNZCGP endorsement will be sought; endorsement has not yet been granted.

Three weeks · three clinics

One changing conversation.

Each live session introduces a problem. Between sessions, participants take a short observation back into practice, then return to reconsider what they noticed.

01 / THE BODY

How did we get here?

Weight-loss medicine, appetite and the problem of willpower. Where has the problem been believed to reside: body, appetite, behaviour, environment—or character?

NOTICE → How do patients and clinicians talk about effort?

02 / THE SUBJECT

When wanting changes

Stigma, desire and the moral economy of weight. Food noise, treatment stigma, deservingness, identity and what patients may be asking weight loss to change.

NOTICE → What lies between what the patient asks for and what matters?

03 / THE CLINIC

Beyond the prescription

Putting biology and biography back together: culture, context, expectations, stigma, goals, agency and the clinician’s own assumptions.

RETURN → What will I do differently in my next consultation?

Why The Social Clinic?

Biology matters.

So do biography, culture, institutions, relationships, stigma, desire and the social world.

Healthcare increasingly has extraordinary capacity to intervene in biology. But biological change occurs in people whose lives cannot be reduced to biomarkers, diagnoses or prescriptions.

Evidence without reduction.
Complexity without obscurity.
Theory that earns its place in practice.

Course director

Dr Andrew Dickson

Social theory made clinically useful.

Andrew is an academic, writer and educator whose work crosses health, organisation studies, sociology and psychoanalytic theory.

His doctoral research examined the psychological and social dimensions of weight loss, appetite and weight-anxiety, including his own experience of pharmacologically mediated weight loss.

He founded The Social Clinic to create rigorous, independent professional education in the parts of clinical practice that biology alone cannot explain.

The aim is not to make clinicians into social scientists. It is to make social science clinically useful.
