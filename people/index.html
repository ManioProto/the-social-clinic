When Wanting Changes

The medication can quieten your appetite. What you do with the quiet is up to you.

GLP-1 medicines can make food feel quieter and hunger feel different. For many people it's the first time weight loss has seemed possible without a fight. It can also knock loose things you didn't know your weight was holding in place: habits, pleasures, relationships, and the story about willpower you've been told since you were a child.

Your body can change faster than your sense of who you are.

The course

The part medicine can cause but can't explain.

When Wanting Changes is a short course for people taking a GLP-1 medicine or thinking about starting one.

It's about the part of the experience medicine can cause but can't explain. There are no diet plans in it, and no drug company money behind it.

What it covers

The things that move when appetite does.

Food noise

Plenty of people describe the constant chatter about food switching off. It's worth asking what that chatter was, and what it was doing for you.

Habit without hunger

Eating was never only about hunger, so the habits don't always leave when the hunger does.

Body and identity

People may start treating you differently. You may not like why.

Stigma and deservingness

Losing weight is somehow only admirable if it hurts. Use a medication and someone will call it cheating, as though suffering were the point.

Relationships

Meals, routines, intimacy and the roles people play at home can all shift when one person's eating changes.

The long haul

Long-term treatment raises questions nobody can answer yet, including how the story ends.

What it won't do

Not medical advice.

I'm interested in everything else.

This course won't tell you whether to take a GLP-1 medicine, which one, what dose, or how to manage it. Those decisions belong to you and a qualified health professional.

About me

Dr Andrew Dickson

I'm Dr Andrew Dickson, an academic and writer working across health, sociology and psychoanalysis. My doctorate examined weight loss, appetite and weight anxiety, including my own experience of losing weight with medication.

The Social Clinic is my attempt to get these ideas out of the university and into the hands of the people they're about.
